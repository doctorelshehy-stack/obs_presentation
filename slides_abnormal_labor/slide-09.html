Uterine Inertia — Clinical Picture & Management

Clinical Picture

  • Prolonged labor
  • Intact membranes
  • Fetus and mother usually not affected (apart from maternal anxiety)
  • More susceptibility for retained placenta and PPH (persistent inertia)
  • Slow cervical dilatation
  • Infrequent and weak uterine contractions

Complications

  • Prolonged 2nd stage → instrumental delivery or CS
  • Maternal exhaustion
  • Retained placenta & PPH (atony)
  • Fetal infection (prolonged ROM)

Management

General Measures

  • Good obstetric exam to detect disproportion/malpresentation
  • Proper management of 1st stage (ambulation, hydration, analgesia)
  • If membranes ruptured: prophylactic antibiotics

Local Measures

  • Amniotomy if dilation >3cm and head engaged (releases prostaglandins, reflex stimulation)
  • Oxytocin augmentation — start at 2 miu/min, increase incrementally until 3 contractions/10 min. Exclude CPD, malpresentation, scarred uterus, grand multipara

Delivery

  • Vaginal + instrumental (forceps/ventouse) if fully dilated & engaged
  • CS if failure, contraindications to oxytocin, or non-reassuring CTG
