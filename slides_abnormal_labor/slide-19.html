Contracted Pelvis — Diagnosis

A) History

  • Past history of trauma or disease causing pelvic contraction (rickets, osteomalacia, poliomyelitis, TB, fractures, orthopedic surgery)
  • Bad obstetric history: Malpresentation or operative deliveries

B) Examination — General

  • Short stature (< 150 cm)
  • Rachitic manifestations
  • Abnormal gait (limping or waddling)

Examination (continued)

Abdominal

  • Pendulous abdomen
  • Scar of CS
  • Malpresentation: face, brow or shoulder
  • Non-engaged head in last 2 weeks (primigravida)

Local — CPD Tests

CPD tests are done if head not engaged in last 2 weeks of pregnancy. Two methods:

  • Abdominal method (Pinard): Dorsal position, thighs flexed & separated. Head grasped by left hand. Two fingers on symphysis to detect overlapping.
  • Abdomino-vaginal method (Muller-Kerr, bimanual): More valuable. Left hand pushes head, right hand does vaginal exam with thumb on symphysis.

Overlapping Grading (Pinard Method)

No disproportion

Head pushed without overlapping

Moderate disproportion

Head touches under surface of finger by 0.5 cm

Severe disproportion

Head pushed down with overhangs on symphysis pubis
