Hypertonic Inertia & Cervical Dystocia

Hypertonic Inertia

Represents reversed polarity where fundal dominance is lacking.

Types

  • Spastic lower uterine segment: Should be dilated & lax but is contracted
  • Colicky uterus: Weak incoordinate contractions

Clinical Picture

  • More common in primigravida
  • Patient in agony with dehydration & ketosis
  • Irregular, painful contractions, no relaxation
  • Slow cervical dilatation
  • Premature rupture of membranes
  • Prolonged labor
  • Fetal and maternal distress

Management

  • General measures: as hypotonic inertia
  • Obstetric analgesia: pethidine or epidural
  • CS indicated for: CPD, fetal distress before full dilation, cervical dystocia

Cervical Dystocia

Definition

Failure of the cervix to dilate within a reasonable time in presence of good regular uterine contractions.

Types

  • Functional (primary): External os fails to dilate despite no organic lesion. Due to lack of softening during pregnancy.
  • Organic (secondary): Cervical stenosis from previous operations or cervical myoma.

Complications

  • Rupture uterus
  • Postpartum hemorrhage (cervical tear)

Management

  • Analgesics or antispasmodics
  • Cesarean section is mostly required
