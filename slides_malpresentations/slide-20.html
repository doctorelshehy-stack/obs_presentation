Methods of Vaginal Breech Delivery

Spontaneous Breech Delivery

  • No traction or manipulation of the infant is used
  • Occurs predominantly in very preterm infants

Assisted Breech Delivery

  • Most common type of vaginal breech delivery
  • Infant allowed to deliver spontaneously up to the umbilicus
  • Then maneuvers initiated for remainder of body, arms, and head

Total Breech Extraction

  • Fetal feet are grasped, entire fetus extracted
  • Should be used only for a noncephalic second twin
  • Should NOT be used for a singleton fetus

Technique and Tips for Assisted Vaginal Breech Delivery

  • Fetal membranes left intact as long as possible (dilating wedge, prevent cord prolapse)
  • Anesthesiologist & pediatrician immediately available
  • Warm towels, forceps, episiotomy supplies ready
  • Ritgen maneuver to reduce perineal pressure
  • Episiotomies often performed (even in multiparous women) to prevent soft tissue dystocia
  • Pinard maneuver may be needed with frank breech (pressure in popliteal space)
  • No traction exerted until fetal umbilicus past the perineum
  • Gentle downward & outward traction until scapula & axilla visible
