Cord Prolapse — Diagnosis, Investigations & Management

Diagnosis

Abdominal Examination

An ill-fitting or non-engaged presenting part should alert to the possibility of cord prolapse.

Vaginal Examination

  • Cord may be seen protruding from the introitus
  • Loops of cord palpated within vaginal canal
  • If cord is pulsating → fetus is alive
  • Occult prolapse: rarely felt on PV; only sign is FHR changes

Investigations

Color Doppler: Loops of cord in front of the presenting part can be visualized.

Management

Cord Presentation

  • Put patient in knee-chest position
  • Prepare for emergency CS

Cord Prolapse — Living Fetus

  • Partially dilated cervix: Immediate CS
  • Fully dilated cervix: Immediate delivery: Forceps (engaged vertex) or CS (non-engaged vertex & other malpresentations)

Cord Prolapse — Dead Fetus

  • Spontaneous delivery is allowed
