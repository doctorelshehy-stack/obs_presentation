Key Takeaways

01 Vomiting

  • HG → fluid/electrolyte imbalance, weight loss
  • Multifactorial: hormonal, H. pylori, allergic
  • Management: diet, pharmacotherapy, IV hydration
  • Terminate if jaundice, encephalopathy, fundus changes

02 Anemia

  • Iron deficiency, megaloblastic, thalassemia
  • Oral/parenteral iron, folate, B12 injections
  • Parenteral IV iron contraindicated in 1st trimester
  • Thalassemia → blood transfusion, CS delivery

03 Cardiac

  • NYHA classification guides management
  • Peak HF risk: 28–32 wks & labor stages
  • Multidisciplinary care, vaginal unless contraindicated
  • Avoid ergot alkaloids in 3rd stage

04 Thromboembolism

  • DVT: Virchow's triad, 50% asymptomatic, Homan's sign
  • UFH or LMWH (enoxaparin) anticoagulation
  • PE: immediate heparin, IVC filter, thrombolysis
  • LMWH advantages: better bioavailability, lower bleeding

05 Hypertension

  • 4 categories: chronic, gestational, superimposed, PE
  • PE: vasospasm, hemoconcentration, proteinuria
  • MgSO₄ for convulsion prophylaxis
  • Delivery: ≥37wks PE, ≥34wks severe features

06 Diabetes

  • GDM screen at 24–28 wks with 50g GCT
  • GDM-A1: diet + exercise; A2: metformin/insulin
  • Insulin dose: 0.7→0.9 u/kg across trimesters
  • Euglycemia during labor to prevent neonatal hypoglycemia

07 Urinary Tract Infection

  • Screen all pregnancies with MSU at booking
  • Asymptomatic bacteriuria: treat 3 days (40% progress otherwise)
  • Pyelonephritis: hospitalize, IV fluids, parenteral broad-spectrum antibiotics 10–14 days
