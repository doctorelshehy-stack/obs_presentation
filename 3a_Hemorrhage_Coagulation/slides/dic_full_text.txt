Disseminated Intravascular Coagulopathy (DIC) 
 
ILOs of this chapter 
- To understand the meaning of DIC 
- To be able to outline variable causes of DIC in a pregnant woman 
- To be able to explain the management of such disorder with pregnancy 
 
Definition: 
A disorder of hemostasis in which intravascular activation of both clotting and 
fibrinolytic systems occurs leading to consumption of coagulation factors and 
platelets.  
Clinical picture:  
-The patient will experience systemic hemorrhage and oozing during operations. 
-Postpartum hemorrhage. 
Etiology: 
Preeclampsia and eclampsia.  
Amniotic fluid embolism. 
Abruptio placenta. 
Vesicular mole. 
IUFD and missed abortion. 
Incompatible blood transfusion. 
Rupture uterus and placenta accreta. 
Investigations: 
Increased prothrombin time, partial thromboplastin time, and fibrin degradation 
products (D-dimer). Platelet counts and fibrinogen are decreased.  
Treatment:  
Treatment of the cause. 
Transfusion of fresh blood, fresh frozen plasma, cryoprecipitate and platelets. 
Anticoagulant.
