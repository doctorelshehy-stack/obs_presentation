Cephalohematomas

Cephalohematoma

These result from bleeding between the periosteum and skull bones, and take the shape of the underlying skull bone.

The possibility of associated injury (such as skull fracture or intracranial bleeding) should be ruled out.

Most cephalohematomas are benign and resolve without problems.

Key Points

• Develops hours or days after birth

• Localized hematoma to one bone, limited by sutures at its edges

• Well-defined edges

• Elastic (does not pit on pressure)

• Disappears within few weeks
