Lower Segment Cesarean Section (LSCS)

Operative Obstetrics

Types (by uterine incision)

  • Lower segment CS: Operation of choice nowadays.
  • Upper segment CS (classical): Median vertical incision through upper uterine segment.

Steps of LSCS

  1. Disinfection of abdominal wall and toweling. Pfannenstiel incision (transverse suprapubic).
  2. Incise loose peritoneum over lower segment transversely; retract downwards with bladder by Doyen’s retractor.
  3. Incise lower uterine segment transversely in the middle; widen to ~10 cm by two fingers or curved scissors (concavity upward to avoid uterine vessels). Rupture membranes.
  4. Extract head slowly with 2 fingers or one blade of forceps below it + moderate fundal pressure. If impacted: Wrigley’s forceps or assistant pushes head up vaginally.
  5. In breech: grasp feet & extract gently. In shoulder: introduce hand, grasp foot, extract as breech.
  6. Cut cord between 2 Kochers; deliver infant; remove placenta & membranes.
  7. Close uterine incision in 2 layers: 1st for deep muscle, 2nd for rest of muscle.
  8. Remove blood & liquor; close abdominal wound in layers.

Upper Segment CS (Classical)

Indications:

  • Placenta previa and accreta
  • Extensive adhesions
  • Large fibroid in lower segment
  • Repaired high VVF or vesicocervical fistula
  • Neglected shoulder presentation
  • If hysterectomy planned after CS
  • Very rapid delivery needed (severe fetal distress, massive APH)
  • Contraction ring

Technical Points — Upper Segment CS

  • Skin: paramedian (1/3 above, 2/3 below umbilicus) or midline subumbilical.
  • Uterus: vertical incision (~12 cm) midway between round ligaments (least vascular area).
  • Buttocks usually encountered; deliver as breech.
  • Close uterus in 3 layers: 1st deep muscle, 2nd main bulk, 3rd superficial muscle + peritoneum.
